The A2 e-tron is built on the Volkswagen Group's **MEB+ platform**, the updated version of the architecture underneath the Volkswagen ID.3 and Cupra Born. Every version is **rear-wheel drive** with a single motor on the rear axle — there is no quattro variant in the launch range.

## Motors

Two permanently excited synchronous machines cover the four power levels:

| Drive unit | Used by | Power | Torque |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| APP350 | 125 kW, 140 kW, 170 kW | up to 170 kW | 350 Nm |
| APP550 | 240 kW | 240 kW | 545 Nm |

### What Audi changed in the APP350

The APP350 in the A2 e-tron is a reworked version of the unit used elsewhere in the group, and Audi says it operates up to **10% more efficiently** than the previous design. The changes are individually small and collectively decisive:

- **Silicon-carbide semiconductors** in the power electronics, cutting switching losses
- **0.2 mm motor laminations** — thinner than before, reducing iron losses
- A **delta-connected stator winding**, shifting the motor's efficient operating band to where the car actually spends its time
- **Low-friction transmission oil**
- A long **10.2:1 reduction ratio**, dropping motor speed at highway cruise

The reduction ratio in particular is an efficiency-first choice. A shorter ratio would improve acceleration; the long one keeps the motor turning slowly on a motorway, which is where a long-range compact car earns or loses its WLTP figure.

## Batteries

Three packs are offered, tied to power level:

| Battery | Gross | Net | Chemistry | Voltage | Configuration | Used by |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| Small | 52 kWh | 50 kWh | LFP | 333 V | 104s2p | 125 kW |
| Medium | 61 kWh | 58 kWh | LFP | 333 V | – | 140 kW |
| Large | 84 kWh | 79 kWh | NMC | 353 V | – | 170 kW, 240 kW |

### LFP packs

The two smaller packs use **lithium iron phosphate** cells in a **cell-to-pack** construction: prismatic cells are bonded directly into the housing rather than assembled into intermediate modules. That raises packaging density and lowers the pack's overall height, which is part of how Audi kept the car to 1,583 mm tall without sacrificing headroom.

LFP contains **no nickel and no cobalt**, is inherently durable, and — unlike nickel-based chemistries — can be charged to **100% every day** without the daily charge limit normally recommended. For an entry-level car that will spend most of its life on a home wallbox, that is a genuinely practical advantage.

The LFP charging curve is also unusually flat, which means the 10–80% times of 24 and 26 minutes are more representative of real charging stops than a high peak figure would be.

### NMC pack

The 84 kWh pack uses **nickel manganese cobalt** cells in a modular construction. It supplies the two most powerful versions, raises system voltage to 353 V and lifts DC charging to 183 kW.

## Charging

| | 52 kWh | 61 kWh | 84 kWh |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Max DC power | 100 kW | 105 kW | 183 kW |
| DC 10–80% | 24 min | 26 min | 29 min |

The charge port sits on the **right rear side** with a **CCS2** connector for European markets. Audi also improved **AC charging efficiency to 89.6%**, up 1.3 percentage points, through revised cooling and control software — a gain that matters far more than it sounds, since AC charging is where most owners will put most of their energy into the car.

### Bidirectional charging

The A2 e-tron can feed energy back out of the traction battery in two ways:

- **Vehicle-to-Load (V2L)** — powers external equipment through a socket in the luggage compartment or an adapter at the charge port
- **Vehicle-to-Home (V2H)** — supplies a compatible home electrical system through an Audi-recommended wallbox

V2H is offered initially in **Germany, Austria and Switzerland**. V2L is a separate option in some markets, including Norway.

## Suspension

The front axle uses **MacPherson struts**, the rear a **multi-link** design — the more sophisticated rear layout, rather than the torsion beam that some MEB cars use at lower power levels.

Three suspension setups are offered:

- **Comfort suspension** — the standard setup, with conventional coil springs and passive dampers
- **Sport suspension** — lowered, and also the setup included in the **efficiency package**
- **Suspension with damper control** — adaptive damping, standard on the 240 kW version and optional elsewhere

Steering is **linear** as standard, with **progressive steering** and its variable ratio available as an option.

## Driving modes and recuperation

Four driving modes are selectable: **comfort**, **balanced**, **efficiency** and **dynamic**.

Recuperation is adjustable across **four levels**. Paddles behind the steering wheel select D1 through D3 for progressively stronger coasting deceleration, and a **B position** enables one-pedal driving where the car brakes to a stop on the accelerator alone.

The A2 e-tron also has a customized electronic stability control calibration developed specifically for the rear-wheel-drive layout.
